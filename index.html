01 — Overview

A New Era of Lunar Exploration

"We are going back to the Moon, and this time, we're going to stay."

The Artemis program is NASA's ambitious initiative to return humans to the lunar surface for the first time since 1972. Named for the twin sister of Apollo in Greek mythology, Artemis is more than a mission — it is a sustained human presence on and around the Moon.

Powered by the Space Launch System — the most powerful rocket ever built — and the Orion spacecraft, Artemis will establish the infrastructure needed to support long-duration missions and, ultimately, journeys to Mars.

02 — Mission Phases

Three Missions.
One Destination.

I
Artemis I
Uncrewed Flight Test

Launched November 2022, Artemis I sent the Orion spacecraft on a 25-day journey around the Moon and back — proving the system is ready for crew. Orion traveled 1.4 million miles through deep space.

✓ Mission Complete
II
Artemis II
First Crewed Lunar Flyby

Four astronauts — including the first woman and first Canadian on a lunar mission — will fly around the Moon on a 10-day journey, venturing farther from Earth than any human since Apollo 17.

⬡ Planned · 2025–26
III
Artemis III
Humans Return to the Surface

Artemis III will land humans near the lunar South Pole for the first time, where water ice in permanently shadowed craters could support a long-term human presence and future Mars transit missions.

◯ Future Mission
03 — Lunar Gateway

A Permanent Station in Lunar Orbit

The Gateway is a small space station that will orbit the Moon, serving as a staging point for surface expeditions and a laboratory for deep space science. Built in partnership with international space agencies, it is humanity's first outpost in deep space.

Unlike the International Space Station, Gateway will remain unoccupied for months at a time — a reusable waypoint for astronauts journeying to and from the lunar surface.

  • Orbit Near-rectilinear halo orbit (NRHO) — 9-day period
  • Partners ESA, JAXA, CSA, and international agencies
  • Mass ~40 metric tons at full configuration
  • First launch Power & Propulsion Element — 2027 target
04 — Science & Purpose

Why the Moon — Why Now

◎
Water Ice at the Poles

Permanently shadowed craters at the lunar South Pole harbor ancient water ice that could be converted into drinking water, oxygen, and rocket propellant — enabling a sustained human presence.

⬡
Proving Ground for Mars

The Moon is three days away. Mars is months. Learning to live and work on the Moon teaches us how to survive in deep space before we attempt humanity's next great voyage to the Red Planet.

✦
Scientific Discovery

The lunar surface is a time capsule of the early solar system. South Pole geology, cosmic ray records, and far-side radio observations offer scientific windows unavailable from Earth.

◈
Global Partnership

More than 20 countries have signed the Artemis Accords — establishing shared norms of responsible, transparent space exploration for the benefit of all humankind.

⊕
Commercial Ecosystem

NASA partners with commercial providers — SpaceX, Blue Origin, and others — for lunar landers and cargo delivery, fostering a new lunar economy and dramatically reducing mission costs.

◯
The Human Imperative

Beyond science and strategy, Artemis represents something timeless: the need to explore, to push beyond the horizon, to ask what lies further out in the darkness between worlds.

Mission Profile

The Journey Around the Moon

A 10-day voyage that takes the crew farther from Earth than any humans since Gene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt departed the lunar surface in 1972. No landing — but a critical demonstration before Artemis III touches down.

Day 1
Launch — Kennedy Space Center, FL
Space Launch System lifts off from Launch Complex 39B, carrying Orion and the crew into Earth orbit before the upper stage fires toward the Moon.
Day 2–3
Trans-Lunar Injection
Orion's European Service Module fires to propel the spacecraft out of Earth orbit and onto a trajectory toward the Moon. The crew performs critical systems checks.
Day 4–5
Lunar Approach & Closest Pass
Orion swings around the far side of the Moon, passing just 8,900 km above the surface — closer than any crewed spacecraft since Apollo 17. The crew loses radio contact temporarily.
Day 6–8
Distant Retrograde Orbit & Return Burn
Orion enters a distant retrograde orbit before the crew performs a powered flyby maneuver, slingshotting back toward Earth on a free-return trajectory.
Day 10
Splashdown — Pacific Ocean
Orion re-enters Earth's atmosphere at 40,000 km/h, deploying parachutes before splashdown off the coast of San Diego. USS San Diego recovers crew and capsule.
